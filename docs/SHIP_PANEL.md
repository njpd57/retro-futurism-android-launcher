# Ship panel and bridge console

Moved from CLAUDE.md. Read before touching `src/ship3d/`, `components/ship/` or `components/console/`.

- **Ship panel** (`settings.shipPanel`, off by default; `settings.shipFps` AHORRO / 30 / 60 / 120): PANEL as a first-person 3D walk through the ship instead of the dashboard grid (`App.vue` mounts `ShipPanel` or `Dashboard`; `[EDITAR]` is hidden in ship mode, Home calls `useShipStore.reset()`). It has **its own WebGL2 engine, `src/ship3d/`, no dependencies and not the `games/wire` one** (the wire wireframe stuttered on the phone and its sheared text drew streaks). Pure and tested: `math.ts` (mat4, view from yaw / pitch, `fovYFor`: a fixed horizontal field so a portrait phone doesn't get tunnel vision, rays, ray-rect / ray-cylinder), `world.ts` (the ship as data, kept in `ship.json` and expanded by `applyWorld`; edit it with the map editor, `docs/SHIP_EDITOR.md`: **convex rooms as polygons extruded to a height**, doorways cut on the line where two rooms meet, props, ceiling lamps, 20 monitors (16 on walls plus four `hung` on the crown over the mess table: the ship's terminal `hub.buscar` / `hub.mother`, zone `'hub'`, and `comedor.rancho` / `comedor.pista`); metres, `(x east, y south)`, bow up, `yaw` 0 north clockwise; **the mess (COMEDOR) is the centre**, a dodecagon (`MESS_A`) laid out like the *Torrens* mess: a round table in a sunken ring, `CHAIRS` chairs (`chairAt`), a crown of monitors with a ring of light over it, the galley, lockers, a dispenser, a traffic cone and the drinking bird (`BIRD`); the bridge dome to the north, COMMS / ENFERMERÍA either side, BODEGA south with CAMAROTES / HIPERSUEÑO off it, then MOTOR and HANGAR), `collide.ts` (circle vs segments, slides, stepped so it can't tunnel), `walk.ts` (`stepWalk`: joystick + look into a body with eased speed, bob), `mesh/` (bakes the ship to one triangle list, one file per piece: see `docs/SHIP_MODELS.md`), `lights.ts` (the 8 nearest lights per frame), `atlas.ts` (monitor text is a **texture**: a canvas atlas, one cell per monitor, repainted only when a reading or the palette changes), `minimap.ts`, `pick.ts` (`pickScreen`: a ray from the tapped pixel against the monitors, hidden by walls but not by crates, usable within `REACH` metres), `glide.ts` (`STANDS`, `glideRoute`: where each room's camera spot is and a route of straight legs through the doorways that stays clear of props, checked for every pair of rooms; `VIAS` route round crates and columns), `cards.ts` (`MONITORS`: which dashboard widgets each monitor shows in focus, `CARDS` the room's own for a monitor with no entry, `monitorWidgets`), `readings.ts` (`roomReadings`: lines and idle / notice / urgent per monitor from a flat `ShipInput`, which `composables/useShipInput.ts` gathers from the stores). GL side, checked by hand and in headless Chrome: `gl.ts` (context, static buffers, `webglcontextlost` / `restored`, palette uniforms), `shaders.ts` (one tint: procedural panel seams from the uv in metres, lamps and monitors light the rooms, distance fades into `--ph-bg`; surfaces are near black, light comes from the lamps). UI in `components/ship/`: `ShipPanel` (loop at AHORRO 60 moving / 10 idle, 30 or 60; pauses on `beforePause`; palette read from `--ph` / `--ph-bg` through `parseColor` and re-read on a phosphor change), `ShipControls` (floating joystick on the left half with `stickAxes`, drag on the right half to look with `aimPixels`, a short touch emits `tap`; each finger by `Touch.identifier`), `ShipMinimap` (tap a room: the camera glides there at `GLIDE_SPEED`; the stick cancels it). Life aboard: `doors.ts` (`stepDoors`: a door leaf per doorway, built into the mesh with the door number in its `emit` slot and slid up by the vertex shader, opens within `DOOR_RANGE`), `crew.ts` (`stationaryAgents`: a figure per app with notifications at the COMMS desks; at the mess table (`SEATS`) the starred contacts first (`CONTACT_SEATS`, with plates), then anonymous `diner`s up to `dinersAt(clock)` (`mess.ts`: `MEALS`, full table at mealtimes; a tap says a `dinerLine`), all `seated` and eating (`poseSeated`, `biteLift`, arms by `elbowFor`); the drinking bird is `birdVertices` (`poseBird(birdTilt(t))`, still under reduced motion); `Roamer`s Jones and the android that wait, then walk `glideRoute`s between rooms, frozen under reduced motion), `figures.ts` (their low-poly bodies, rebuilt each frame into a second dynamic buffer through `setCrew`; the crew shader draws dark bodies with a rim of light), `pickAgent` (tap a signal figure: COMMS card; a contact: COMEDOR card; Jones and Ash answer with a line), rooms with a notice or urgent reading light their lamps brighter (urgent blink) and a scan band sweeps the hull every 16 s (`uSweep`); its sounds go through `useSoundStore.play`, so only if the user enabled UI sound, `ShipMonitor` (a tap on a monitor within reach takes the camera up to it: see "Focus mode" below; it is registered in `overlayOpen` / `closeOverlays` so back leaves it), a `[II]` pause button. Render scale is capped at 1.5 device pixels per CSS pixel; without WebGL2 the panel says so and the grid is the fallback. The tap's curvature correction is done by hand (`correctPoint`), because `CrtScreen` doesn't correct canvas touches. Optional bloom (`settings.shipBloom`, off by default, strength from `effective.bloom`): `bloom.ts` (pure, tested: blur weights, half size, gain) and, in `gl.ts`, the frame goes to a multisampled framebuffer, is resolved to a texture, its bright part is cut out at half size, blurred both ways and screen-blended back on the level from `--ph-bg` to `--ph`, so it stays one tint; gain 0 draws straight to the screen and frees the buffers, and a device whose framebuffers won't complete just goes without. Done: engine, ship, monitors, joystick, look, collisions, minimap, cards, glide, pause, doors, crew, sweep, alert lights, sounds, bloom.
- **Bridge console** (our status bar, `components/console/`, `settings.ownStatusBar`): line 1 sits in the status bar inset and is split by the camera, whose width comes from `getDisplayCutoutPath()` or `settings.cameraWidth` (`cameraGap` in `utils/statusBar.ts`): telemetry on the left (`TelemetryRotor`: one reading at a time, rotating every 4 s in one blink: the compass heading `RUMBO 090 E` from `useHeading`, a simulated pulse drawn as a cardiac monitor sweep (`monitorSweep`: a cursor writes the trace left to right), the weather and the storage fill; no clock, the tab bar has it; a tap moves on), `MotherEye` around the camera (the camera is the pupil: two brackets that wink on a new signal, blink while MOTHER answers; tap opens MOTHER) and the `Annunciator` lamps `COM PWR MSG ALM PRT` on the right (inverse when lit, blinking when urgent; MSG opens the notifications, the others put their line first on the tape). Line 2 is MOTHER's `Teletype` (`settings.ticker`): one character per step, the tape reloaded only between passes; it names a notification's app, never its title or text, and carries the battery (`RESERVA 74 %`). Pure logic in `utils/bridgeConsole.ts` (`annunciators`, `tickerQueue`, `advance`, tested); `useBridgeConsoleStore` gathers the stores and chirps when a lamp lights up.
- **Ship panel as a launcher** (v3, in `src/ship3d/` and `components/ship/`): the walk does real work. `hud.ts` + `ShipHud` (the strip with the room and its key reading; a tap opens the RUTA menu of every room; `nextAlertRoom` is the chip that glides to the loudest other room, with `turnToward` for the arrow), name plates (`labels.ts`: the atlas has a band under the monitors, `LABEL_SLOTS` plates, `plateTexts` / `withStencils`; `MAT.label` quads that the shader draws as floating letters only, `plateVertices` for the ones over the crew's heads and `plate()` in `mesh.ts`), the crew of COMMS is one figure per `radarContacts` app and a tap opens `SignalScanner` (the radar's scanner, shared; `useShipStore.signals`, an overlay in `overlayOpen` / `closeOverlays`), starred contacts open their card (never a call), the HANGAR shuttles are `settings.favorites` (`hangarBays`, `shuttleVertices` in the dynamic buffer, collidable through `collisionSegments`, `pickShuttle`; a tap goes through `useLaunchStore.launch`), and the hub's terminal monitors `hub.buscar` (`useShipStore.askSearch` -> `App.vue` focuses the prompt) and `hub.mother` (switches tab). `settings.shipStart` picks where Home leaves the camera. **Crew as phosphor silhouettes:** `pose.ts` (pure: `posePerson` walk / idle / typing, `poseCat`, `headTurn`), `figures.ts` draws the posed boxes with uv 0..1 per face so the shader traces their edges, adds a rim and climbing scan lines (`uHolo`, still under reduced motion), a busy app's figure (>= `HEAVY_CONTACT` signals) blinks with `uBlink`. **Look of the ship (v4):** smooth gradients alone read as plastic, and thin procedural lines shimmered, so the surfaces use **real detail textures** and the edges are **drawn in post, per pixel**. Detail: `public/ship/{wall,floor,ceiling,prop}.png` (512 px RGB: brightness, normal x, normal y; made by `scripts/pack_ship_textures.py` from the ambientCG CC0 materials listed in `ASSETS.md`, which must list the source and licence of any asset added; CC-BY / CC-BY-NC are fine for this private personal project), loaded by `ShipPanel` and uploaded by `gl.setDetail` with mipmaps and anisotropy (flat until they arrive); the fragment shader reads them with `detail()` and bends the normal through `cotangentFrame` (derivatives of position and uv, constant per face, so no shimmer). **Light and occlusion are baked into the vertices** (`bake.ts`, pure and tested: `bakeVertex` gives `ao` 1 in open air and lower at the foot of walls, in corners, at the ceiling and on the floor round crates; `lit` = the lamps of the vertex's own room, stronger in tall rooms; `dir` = where that light mostly comes from, so a normal-mapped surface answers to it) and that needs geometry cut into cells: `tessellate` cuts floors and ceilings along a 1 m world grid (pieces clipped to the room so neighbours share points) and `wallGrid` the walls, about 50 000 vertices (a test caps it at 70 000). `Builder.room` says which room what is being built stands in; set it before adding anything to the static mesh (no room = no baked light). `STRIDE` is 15 (`aAo`, `aLit`, `aDir` at the end). The shader adds `lit` (turned by the relief and `dir`) to 40 % of the frame's 8 nearest lights, which is what makes a room that wants attention brighten or blink without lamps popping in and out, plus a Blinn-Phong highlight per surface kind (`shin`, `ks`) so metal does not look like clay. **Pipeline (`gl.ts`):** the scene is drawn into our own framebuffer with two outputs (the picture, and the distance in three bytes plus the surface kind), no multisampling (the canvas is already drawn at twice the CSS size, `renderScale`), then the composite pass (`COMPOSITE_FRAGMENT`, highp: the distance must come back whole) finds outlines where 1 / depth stops being a straight line across the screen (silhouettes and creases) or the surface kind changes, adds the glow if `shipBloom` is on and a fixed grain; a device whose framebuffers won't complete draws straight to the screen without outlines. A room's style still rides in the material number as `mat + 16 * style` (`STYLE`, `MAT_STYLE`) but only sets `wallBase` brightness. Anything thinner than a pixel must fade out with `fwidth` (monitor scan lines, hazard stripes and the crew's climbing lines do), never `step()` or a thin `lines()`. To tune the look, render it: `scripts/ship-shot.sh` (see `docs/SHIP_MODELS.md`) draws the ship from a given camera in headless Chrome and saves a PNG. `MAT.hazard` chevrons at the working rooms' doors and under shuttles, wall stencils (`STENCILS`, `stencilSpot`), corridor ribs and pipes with quilted cushions between the ribs (`CUSHIONS`) and hatch plates down the floor, bridge keyboards, a captain's chair, mess lockers; monitors have curved glass in the shader (the atlas cell is found from the uv). The render scale is 2 (1 on low battery) and the panel lowers the tube's scan lines through `--scan-alpha` while it is mounted. The mesh has a vertex budget (a test caps it at 1.4x the plain ship). **Sound:** new `ship*` names in `sfx.ts` (UI channel, in the mixer), and a hum per room (`acoustics.ts` pure: `VOICES`, `hear` = gain and pan from the path of doorways, open ones let more through; `audio/shipAmbient.ts` one voice per zone; `useSoundStore.setShip` / `listenShip`, 10 Hz; the terminal hum drops to 40 % while it runs; `settings.shipRoomSound`). **Battery:** `power.ts` (`lowPower` under 20 % unplugged: 30 fps, render scale 1, no bloom, `AHORRO` in the HUD). Settings: `shipLabels`, `shipLook`, `shipInvertY`. Don't touch the games from here: the ship has its own sounds and no longer uses AISLAMIENTO's `step` / `door`.

- **Colour (v5, the clean Torrens look):** the ship is drawn in colour; only the monitors and the holographic crew stay phosphor (`phosphor` in the fragment shader, `mix(uBg, uPh, level)`). Each material in `materials.json` has a `color` (`#rrggbb`, `uColor[16]`) and every room has its kind of light: `styleLamps` in `materials.json`, one per style (`zoneStyle`; `Builder.vertex` stamps the room's style on everything built in it), warm in the mess, amber in the engine room, cold in hypersleep; a `styled` material (the walls) also takes `styleColors` and `styles` (brightness) from its room. An urgent room's light blinks into alarm red (`GLFrame.alerts`, `uStyleAlert`); light sources are boosted 3x so they read as lights against pale walls. A glow prop that names a material shines in its colour (status cells, dispenser tips). `settings.shipColor` = `phosphor` (`GLFrame.color = false`, `uMono`) paints the whole ship as the luminance of the colour picture in the phosphor's tint; the composite and glow passes work in RGB in colour mode (outlines become a faint dark crease). Textures are **drawn, not scanned**: `scripts/gen_ship_panels.py` makes `panel` (moulded panel with an octagonal bead), `plain`, `quilt`, `floor`, `ceiling` (coffers), `louvre`, `mesh` and `door` (X mouldings and a hexagonal window), one architectural module per tile. Against repetition, `detail()` makes each repeat a touch lighter or darker (`hash12` of the tile cell), a slow world-space `valueNoise` varies the colour, and the relief flattens where the texture is finer than the pixels. Rooms can name `floor` / `wall` / `ceiling` materials in `ship.json`. Doorways (`mesh/doorway.ts`: `addDoorway`, `wallRing`, `wallBox`, `wallGlow`) have the Nostromo's stepped white surround lined in orange, a three-cell status box and tube lights where the wall has room; corridors (`mesh/corridor.ts`) have a sloped sill with vents, ribs with stepped orange cushions, a cornice and a strip of light. The mess (`messProps`, `chairProps`) has a white table with an orange rim on a mesh drum, a turntable with jars, yellow chairs on dark tube and a hood; the galley, lockers, bench and posters are props with materials in `ship.json`. Printed signs (`signs` in `ship.json`: `LECHE`, `MANTEQUILLA`… over the galley's dispensers; `SIGNS`, `SIGN_PLATES`, `withSigns`, `addSigns`) are atlas plates that always show; in colour the label shader draws them as white ink on a dark strip (emit 0.75) and the room stencils as dark paint (emit 0.5), while plates of light (crew, shuttles, emit 1) stay phosphor. Cylinders can have `sides` (the mess hood is an octagon turned by `yaw`). The other rooms take the kit through materials in `ship.json`: graphite bridge consoles with rows of coloured lit keys (`addKeyboards`), white infirmary beds, orange and mustard cargo, bunks with orange padding, white hypersleep pods with a lid of cold light (`pods`), the engine core in amber with dark rings. `src/shot.ts` paints the stencils and signs and takes `&mono=1`.

- **Stations (v6: focus mode, controls, seats; plan phases 0-4).** The ship is the interface: monitors are used where they hang and the phone's settings are switches on panels.
  - **Focus mode.** A tap on a monitor in `REACH` sets `useShipStore.focus` (a screen id). `ShipPanel` moves the camera there with `camera.ts` (pure: `Shot`, `focusShot` = straight in front of the glass so it fills `FOCUS_FILL` of the view, `tweenShot` eased over `SHOT_SECONDS`, instant with reduced motion, `glassCorners` = the glass's four corners in CSS pixels) and lays the monitor's widgets on the glass as **real HTML**: `ShipMonitor.vue` is a box the size of the glass whose `transform` is `quadTransform(w, h, corners)` (`utils/quadTransform.ts`, pure: the projective map from a box to four points as a `matrix3d`), recomputed each frame while the camera moves and when the canvas changes size (the keyboard). So scrolling, `TermInput` and taps work as in the dashboard. Its atlas cell is painted empty while it is in focus. The walk's joystick, minimap, HUD and `[II]` step aside and `[« VOLVER]` leaves; back (Android) does too. Never put a panel or prop between the focus camera and a monitor's glass: the HTML would draw over it (this happened with a raised bridge console).
  - **Quick settings.** `useQuickSettingsStore` (flashlight, brightness and auto brightness, ringer, media volume, night mode, Wi-Fi / Bluetooth / location read-only, `openPanel`, `grant`): fork methods guarded with `bridgeHas()`, state from the `*Changed` events, permissions re-read on `afterResume`. Brightness needs "Modify system settings" and the ringer "Do Not Disturb access" (both in `AJUSTES › PERMISOS`); night mode only has its permission through adb. Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and location can't be changed by an app: their lamps open the system's panel.
  - **Panels and controls** are data in `ship.json`: `panels` (a plate at `(x, y, z)` facing `yaw`, leaning `tilt`) and `controls` (`panel`, `u` / `v` on its face, `kind`: `toggle`, `lever`, `selector`, `button`, `lamp`, `gauge`; `action`; `label`). `world.ts` exports `PANELS` / `CONTROLS`. `mesh/controls.ts` bakes the slab, its bezels and the name plates (`CONTROL_PLATES` in `labels.ts`, laid flat on the face); `controls.ts` (pure) draws the moving parts each frame into the dynamic buffer (`controlVertices`: bat, lever handle, selector pointer, button cap sunk while pressed, lamps, dial needle; a locked control has a blinking red lamp) and holds `tapValue` / `dragValue` / `stepShown`. `pickControl` (in `pick.ts`) finds the control under a finger; a lever dragged takes the finger (`ShipControls` `grab`, `grabMove`, `grabEnd`) instead of the walk or the look. What each action reads and does is `actions.ts` (pure: `controlState`, `voiceLine`, `readout`, `liveLabel`, `LOCK_TEXT`) fed by `composables/useShipControls.ts`, which also does `apply` (calls the store), `sleep` / `wake`. A control always shows the phone's real value; a value just set is shown until the phone answers (1.5 s). A locked control says why (`ACCESO DENEGADO · ...`) and offers `[CONCEDER]`. New sounds `shipSwitch`, `shipKey`, `shipLever`, `shipDenied`; `settings.shipVoice` (off) makes MOTHER confirm aloud; `settings.haptics` gives a tick. To add a control: a panel and a control in `ship.json` and, if it is a new action, a case in `controlState` and `apply` (and `voiceLine` if she should say it). The panels: PUENTE two arm panels by the captain's seat (LUCES CABINA, AUTO, FOCO EXT. / ALERTA, VOLUMEN, NOCTURNO), COMMS `comms.enlaces` (ENLACE, BALIZA, POSICIÓN, DATOS), COMEDOR `comedor.radio` (ATRÁS, RADIO, AVANCE), CAMAROTES `camarotes.reloj` (the next alarm on its plate), MOTOR `motor.diales` (RESERVA, CARGA, ALMACÉN) and HANGAR `hangar.consola` (the first favourite app's shortcuts, only with Bridge as the default launcher). The `SignalScanner` of COMMS also has `RESPONDER` (a `TermInput` and `[TRANSMITIR]`, through `requestReplyToNotification`).
  - **Stations (seats).** `stations` in `ship.json` (`StationData`: eye at `(x, z, y)`, `yaw`, `arc` either way, pitch limits, `r` / `h` of the tap target, `lying`): the captain's seat and the six hypersleep capsules. A tap on one (`pickStation`) sets `useShipStore.seat`; `camera.ts` `stationShot` / `clampLook` hold the eye and the look inside the station's arc; the whole screen looks (`ShipControls` `lookOnly`), no minimap or pause, `[LEVANTARSE]` stands. A monitor can be focused while seated and `[« VOLVER]` returns to the seat; back leaves the monitor first, then the seat. **Lying in a capsule** puts the phone to sleep (`useShipControls.sleep`: silent ringer and brightness 8 %, saving what was there) and standing up restores it (`wake`).
  - **The ship answers the phone.** The flashlight adds a floodlight over the bridge to the frame's lights, brightness (`quick.brightness`, manual) dims what the lamps light through `GLFrame.dim` / `uDim` (never the lamps themselves), and a silent or vibrating ringer lights the amber cell of every doorway's status box (`QUIET_CELLS` in `mesh/doorway.ts`, `quietVertices` in `controls.ts`).
